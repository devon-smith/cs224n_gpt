Thine eyes I love, and they, as pitying me,
Knowing thy heart torment me with disdain,
Have put on black, and loving mourners be,
Dressed in oblivion, and dressed to o'er times,
As show of thy merciful love;
All this shall owe thy prayers:
Doubt decay down my writ, and steal thy heart.
Doth breed grief where decay gives pleasure;
But never by thine living love shall beauty vout child's darlings;
O'er orphans keep thy part, and, seeing shame,
Am drained all youth into weeds.
  Save thee, thou remembrance'st bred that virtuous love lived:
  And seek whose goodness may make thee dear to spite.

Beshrew that heart that makes my heart to groan
For that deep wound it gives my friend and me.
Is 't not enough to torture me alone,
With such an appetite as desires,
I go one step toward joy?
Be that passion that spoils myself now—never forget
When is when grief ne'er endures me,
Within my sense, with wrinkles out of frost,
A hundred ranks ere I am no more.
At the needs of public and lofty dignities,
O my beloved creature grant joy and shun against me,
Which short measure isn't to be confined,
Thrifts in myself and in anyone else;
  More, when wards are provided to it,
  I give to all my owners such assistance.

Thou blind fool, Love, what dost thou to mine eyes
That they behold and see not what they see?
They know what beauty is, see where it lies, but to tell
Who this beauty lies, it thought they hath read.
All looks him alike; for him either they are alone or none.
By that which cold proves life, but to look age's bright,
Flutters aside the more precious mountain.
A view so worthy
As any either of now or every eternity;
Doth fled without time am of Lacedaemon,
And in his true youth lends his pity.
  All unseen things perceive it in that,
  and to follow it the night they see feel with fading eyes.

8
Be wise as thou art cruel; do not press
My tongue-tied patience with too much disdain,
Lest sorrow lend me words, and words express ill purpose,
If, enduring thoughts locked in a black pencil.
When a dull affliction calls him thee mad,
He takes things imprudent with a feeling worse,
Of which found strength in envy and affliction;
Be assured of an effect greater,
Than not of lack but of amity; try to cure
Thrangling ills like vexation, with thine pen dare.
  Farewell, though mine eyes, all happy,
  Perhaps worst mendin' all this.

Lo, as a careful huswife runs to catch
One of her feathered creatures broke away,
Sets down her babe, and makes all swift dispatch.
By springs leaving? Their times passed worse,
Than 'gainst woe to see no bounty,
Neither to water rearing or hauling or store nor rent
Of nearly all their import.
Or rather sight, silent as their watch finds
In the bird aloft mute, then silent for some time,
Shall hang before even brief company, but his prize is never wasted.
  Although hence might then come sight before all fortune—
  Leap on away, and all to 'gainst appearance rest alone.
